## 1. The licence

For a typeface sold with toppings, the licence is for **ordinary design work**: using the fonts, on the Licensee's computers, to make designs — print, packaging, signage, presentations, documents, static graphics — for the Licensee's own projects and for its clients' projects.

- **Licensee:** the person or company named at purchase, within the size it declared (people who work for it). One licence per organisation size.
- **Not covered by the licence itself** (each is a topping below): a brand's logo, a website, social media, an app or other embedding, broadcast and advertising.
- **Never covered:** passing the font files to anyone (except a Subcontractor, §3), selling or sublicensing them, putting them in a font tool or a service that sets text for others, making a font from them.

## 2. Brand licences (toppings)

Bought **per brand and per style**: the buyer names the brand (a client, or the Licensee's own brand), the brand's size, the uses, and the styles used. A brand licence covers only those styles, for that brand.

| Use | Covers | Bands |
|---|---|---|
| **Logo** | The brand's logo or wordmark, set in the font or drawn from it. | 1 logo · up to 3 logos |
| **Web** | The brand's own website(s), by web font. | up to 100K page views a month |
| **Social** | The brand's social media posts and profiles. | — |
| **App** | Embedding in an app, a game, an e-book, or software that sets text on a server. | — |
| **Broadcast** | Film, television, streaming and advertising. | — |

- **Size of the brand** is its own headcount, not the studio's, and is declared by the buyer. A wrong declaration is a breach of the licence. *(counsel: consequence and wording)*
- A brand licence may only be bought for a style the Licensee already holds a licence for.
- A brand may buy more uses, or more styles, later, at the prices then in force.
- A logo remains the brand's; the licence covers the font's part in it only. *(counsel: trademark, "font used as logo" wording)*

## 3. Subcontractors

A flat fee per subcontractor (a freelancer, a printer) lets the Licensee share the font files with that one party, **only to produce the Licensee's own work**, who must delete them when the job is done. Any number of styles.

## 4. Updates, for good

- Every style bought carries **every later version of that style, for good**, through the buyer's download link.
- **Complete Collection** carries every style the family has, **including styles added later** (and, in early access, the styles not yet drawn).
- A single style bought on its own carries that style's updates only; styles added later are separate.
- A later version may change shapes, spacing and features; earlier versions stay valid under the licence they were bought with.

## 5. Early access

- A family on sale before it is finished says so on the page: its stage (Early access, Beta, Release candidate) and version.
- Its price is lower the less finished it is, and rises as the stage advances. A purchase made at any stage keeps every update.
- A style not yet drawn can be had only as part of the Complete Collection, and arrives when it is drawn. *(counsel: what is promised, and a date or none)*

## 6. Delivery

By a download link tied to the payment (no account). The link is the buyer's key: it is not to be shared. A lost link is re-sent to the email of the payment.

## Open points for counsel

1. EU consumers: the right of withdrawal for digital content (consent at download), and the refund policy for early access.
2. The wording of a wrong size declaration, and of a brand licence "for a client" when the client later changes.
3. "Ordinary design work" — where it ends: video titles, e-book covers, merchandise and packaging produced in volume are the grey areas; say them in the text or move them to a topping.
4. Whether a studio's **client** needs anything for the printed matter the studio delivers (the draft says no).
5. Retiring the Project License on `/licensing` and in the EULA, and the Legal Notice's "download link within 24 hours" (no longer true once delivery is by link).
